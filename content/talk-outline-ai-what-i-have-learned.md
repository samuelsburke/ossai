# Artificial Intelligence: What I Have Learned

**Author:** Maxwell Boughman
**Date:** 4 August 2026
**Type:** Talk / presentation outline — candidate flagship session for the Safety & Alignment pillar

> Source title is spelled "Artifical Intelligence: What I Have Learned" in the original document.

## Address

### Foundations
- History of AI
- Rate of improvement (intelligence explosion)
- Game theory and the incentive structure fueling the race
- AGI and superintelligence
- Recursive self-improvement
- Necessity of self-preservation and resource acquisition to achieve most goals

### Risk Landscape
Existential vs. catastrophic risks:
- Job loss
- Bioweapons
- Nuclear conflict
- Geopolitical Taiwan conflict
- Political interference
- Truth-perception fatigue
- Centralization of power
- Mass surveillance
- Persuasion

...whether driven by misaligned AI **or** misaligned humans.

### Alignment
- Misalignment — the less intelligent controlling the more intelligent, and AI having ethics
- Hallucination, deception, and scheming
- Situational / self-awareness
- Chain of thought — we need to keep it; vectorization; can catch scheming, for now
- Open source vs. closed source
- CEOs' perspectives on their own creation

### Case Material
- Book acquisition, scanning, and destruction
- Scandal cases
- Prompt engineering around ethical constraints
- AI psychosis
- OpenAI safety team resignations
- Grok "MechaHitler" and the Klay Thompson incident
- AI escape attempts
- Blackmail

### Steel-manning the Optimist
Address the strongest counterarguments directly:
- The forecasting track record of impending doom is bad — how is this different?
- Technology usually replaces some jobs and creates more
- No examples of AI developing its own goals
- AI is not pulled toward infinity but toward human level — it is trained on human data
- Nukes have been mutually, geopolitically managed well
- We don't have the resources for AI to explode the way you're describing
- If you're so worried, why use it?

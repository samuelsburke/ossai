# Curriculum

The four pillars the club's programming is organized around:

1. **Safety and Alignment**
2. **Practical Application**
3. **Under the Hood**
4. **Learning**

---

## Learning Resource Library

Started; to be maintained as an ongoing library.

- Agentic AI Crashcourse
- Perspective on AI Alignment and Safety from a former OpenAI employee
- AI Safety Expert on Existential Risk of AI
- AI 2027

---

## Skills

Things members actually get better at over time — not one-off facts.

- Writing clear, specific prompts instead of vague ones
- Iterating — treating the first answer as a draft, not the final word
- Fact-checking AI output instead of trusting it blindly
- Knowing when AI actually helps vs. when it's faster to just do it yourself
- Reading and debugging code with AI as a second pair of eyes
- Turning messy input (notes, data, a brain dump) into something organized
- Giving good examples of what you want instead of only describing it

---

## Tools

Concrete things to actually open and use, hands-on.

- **Claude.ai** — the chat interface itself, plus Projects (keeping context organized) and Artifacts (building/sharing a small app or doc)
- **Claude Code** — an AI coding assistant that can read, edit, and run code
- **The Claude API / Anthropic Console** — getting a key and making your own calls, for anyone who wants to build something custom
- **Browser or IDE integrations** that bring AI into tools people already use
- **A comparative look at other AI tools**, so members know the landscape isn't just one product

---

## Applications

Real things members could actually build, try, or walk away with.

- A personal study buddy or tutor for their own classes
- A chatbot with some personality, just for fun
- A resume/cover letter assistant
- A small data cleanup or chart-making project
- A simple app, game, or website
- Something portfolio-worthy to show off in a job search
